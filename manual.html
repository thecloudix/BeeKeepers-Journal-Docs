1. Quick start

Beekeeper's Journal is a fully offline notebook for managing your hives. There are no accounts, no ads, and no tracking. Everything stays on your device unless you choose to export a backup.

  1. Add your first hive. On the home screen, tap the + icon in the top bar. Give the hive a name (it'll later show on calendars, QR labels, and reminders).
  2. Log an inspection. Open a hive from the list, then tap Log inspection for this hive. Fill in what you observed and tap Save inspection.
  3. Schedule the next check. From the home screen Quick actions, tap Schedule next inspection, pick a date, check the hives the reminder applies to, then tap Schedule. A local notification fires on that day for each selected hive.
  4. Back up your data periodically. Open Settings → Data backup → Export backup. You'll get a ZIP you can put in cloud storage or email yourself.

2. Home screen

The home screen lists every hive you've added. Tap a hive's card to open its detail page.

Both the Hives list and the Quick actions list are collapsible — tap a section header to fold it shut, tap again to reopen. Useful if you have a lot of hives and want quick actions in reach without scrolling. The collapsed state is remembered across app launches.

Quick actions

Below the hive list:

  • Scan hive QR — opens the camera scanner to read a printed hive QR. Lands you straight on that hive's detail page.
  • Schedule next inspection — pick a date and check the hives the reminder should apply to. Lets you set the same reminder across many hives at once instead of one at a time. Any existing reminder on a selected hive is replaced.
  • Log inspection — opens the inspection form (you'll pick a hive inside).
  • Log harvest — opens the harvest form.
  • Feed recipes — opens the recipe list with the built-in calculator.
  • Disease library — opens the curated disease reference.
  • Upcoming reminders (count) — pending treatment doses and scheduled inspection reminders, in one inbox. The number badge updates live.
  • View hives on map — shows every hive that has a location pin on a map.

3. Hives

Each hive has its own detail page with four tabs and a row of "Log …" buttons at the top.

Top bar

  • Back — return to home.
  • Calendar — opens the per-hive month calendar.
  • Settings — opens this hive's settings (rename, QR label, location, delete).

Log buttons

The top of the page has four elevated buttons:

  • Log inspection for this hive
  • Log harvest for this hive
  • Log feeding for this hive
  • Log treatment for this hive

If a next-inspection reminder is set for this hive (from the home screen Quick action), the date is shown as a small line under the log buttons.

Tabs

Inspections / Harvests / Feedings / Treatments. Each shows the count and lists every record for this hive. Tap a row to open its detail screen; the overflow menu on each row offers a Delete with confirmation.

Hive Settings

The gear icon opens Hive Settings, which has four sections:

  • Hive name — rename the hive. Save name is enabled when the text differs from the current name.
  • QR label — see QR labels.
  • Location — pick a pin on a map, use your current location, or look up an address. The pin is private to your device.
  • Danger zone — Delete this hive — permanently removes the hive plus every inspection, photo, harvest, feeding, treatment record, and scheduled reminder attached to it. A confirmation dialog warns first. This can't be undone.

4. Inspections

An inspection is the canonical record of "what I saw inside hive X on day Y."

Logging a new one

  1. Tap Log inspection for this hive (or the home Log inspection quick action and pick a hive).
  2. Fill in the form. Every field is optional except the hive.
  3. Tap Save inspection.

Editing an inspection

From the inspection detail page, tap the pencil Edit icon in the top bar. The same form opens with all fields and photos pre-loaded. Changes — including adding or removing photos — are saved when you tap Save changes. Removing a photo deletes the file from your device immediately.

Deleting

Inspection detail's trash icon (or the row's overflow menu on a hive's Inspections tab) deletes the inspection and its photos with a confirmation.

5. Harvests

A harvest records what came out of a hive — honey, pollen, wax, propolis, or anything else you type.

  1. Tap Log harvest from home or Log harvest for this hive from a hive.
  2. Pick the hive, the type, an amount, and a unit (lb, kg, frames, etc.).
  3. Save. The entry shows in the hive's Harvests tab and on the hive calendar.

Tap a harvest row to view detail; the trash icon deletes it.

6. Feed recipes & feedings

Open Feed recipes from the home quick actions. Four curated recipes ship with the app:

  • 1:1 sugar syrup (spring / summer stimulative)
  • 2:1 sugar syrup (fall winter-prep)
  • Winter sugar block / candy board (winter emergency)
  • Pollen substitute patty (early-spring build-up)

Built-in calculator

Each recipe's detail screen lists ingredients with editable quantity fields. Change any ingredient and the others scale automatically to keep the ratio. Units come from your Settings choice (US: lb/oz, cups/pints/gal · Metric: g/kg, mL/L). Water-like ingredients display by volume; dry ingredients by weight.

Tap the refresh icon in the top bar to reset to the recipe's default 1× amounts.

Logging a feeding

  1. Adjust the ingredient amounts to what you actually mixed.
  2. Tap Log this feeding.
  3. Pick the hive (pre-selected if you arrived via Log feeding for this hive).
  4. Pick the date (defaults to today; can be back-dated or future-dated).
  5. Add optional notes and tap Save feeding.

The feeding appears on the hive's Feedings tab and as a day-dot on the hive calendar.

7. Treatments

The treatment library is curated against the Mann Lake Varroa chart plus reference protocols for AFB, Nosema, Small Hive Beetle, Wax Moth (Para-Moth, Certan, frame freezing), and Tracheal Mites (menthol crystals; Formic Pro and MAQS also treat tracheal mites at the standard Varroa dose). DWV draws on the existing Varroa protocols since DWV has no direct treatment — controlling Varroa is the management.

Starting a treatment

  1. From the home screen, open Disease library (or from a hive's page tap Log treatment for this hive, which pre-selects the hive throughout the flow).
  2. Pick a disease (e.g. Varroa Mites) and tap Available treatments.
  3. Pick a protocol (e.g. Apivar, Apiguard, Oxalic acid vapor).
  4. Review the dosing schedule, temperature range, honey-super safety note, and any usage notes.
  5. Tap Start treatment for hive…. Pick the hive, pick a start date (today or any future date), add an optional note, and tap Start or Schedule.

What happens on start

  • If the start date is today: dose 1 is recorded as applied right now. Doses 2..N (if the protocol is multi-dose) become pending at the protocol's interval.
  • If the start date is in the future: dose 1 becomes pending at that date and time. Doses 2..N follow the interval from there.
  • For every pending dose, a local reminder is scheduled. You'll get a notification on the scheduled day.

Confirming or cancelling pending doses

Open Upcoming reminders from the home screen. Pending doses appear with two buttons:

  • Confirm — marks the dose as applied at the current time. It moves onto the hive's calendar and into the Treatments tab.
  • Cancel — removes the dose entirely (with a confirmation). The reminder won't fire and the dose won't appear in any history.

You'll also see the next-inspection reminders here when one is set; tap Open hive or Dismiss.

Reference only

Treatment summaries are educational — many products are regulated and require veterinary or extension authorization. Always follow product labels and your local rules.

8. Disease library

The curated library covers common bee diseases and pests with reference photos where licensing allows. Current entries: Varroa Mites, Small Hive Beetle, Chalkbrood, Wax Moth, Deformed Wing Virus, Sacbrood, American Foulbrood, European Foulbrood, Tracheal Mites, and Nosema. Sources include USDA ARS, Penn State Extension, Oklahoma State Extension, and the Alberta Government.

  • Search by name, symptom, or pest.
  • Filter by severity and life stage.
  • Tap an entry for the full detail page: scientific name, symptoms, treatment options, reference photos (tap to zoom), source attribution, and a disclaimer.
  • Available treatments on the disease page opens the curated treatment protocols for that disease.

You can also open the library from the inspection form (the Reference disease library button), where tapping a disease appends its name to the observations field.

9. Hive calendar

The calendar icon on a hive's page opens a per-hive month view. Days with any record are dotted. Inspections, harvests, feedings, and applied treatment doses all show. Pending treatment doses do not appear here — they live in Upcoming reminders until you confirm them.

  • Tap a day to drill in to the entries on that day.
  • Tap a card on the day view to open that entry's detail screen.
  • The Today icon in the top bar jumps back to today.

10. Reminders & notifications

The app sends two kinds of local notifications. Local means the notification is generated on your device — there is no push server.

Treatment-dose reminders

When you start a multi-dose treatment, each follow-on dose is scheduled. The device fires a notification on the scheduled date.

Next-inspection reminders

The Schedule next inspection Quick action on the home screen lets you pick a date and check one or more hives. The device fires a notification on that day for each selected hive. Scheduling for a hive that already has a reminder replaces the previous one.

What time does the reminder fire?

Both kinds fire at the same time of day you set them. If you scheduled at 3:14 PM today, the reminder fires at 3:14 PM on the chosen day. WorkManager (Android's job scheduler) can defer the actual firing by a few minutes — up to about 15 — when the device is in deep Doze. For "remind me on Thursday" that's invisible; for exact-minute precision, this app doesn't support that yet.

Permission

On Android 13+ the app requests POST_NOTIFICATIONS on first launch. If you deny it, schedules are still kept locally — pending doses stay in Upcoming reminders, the inspection date stays in Hive Settings — but no banner appears.

Channels

Two notification channels exist so you can mute one independently in Android Settings → Apps → Beekeeper's Journal → Notifications. Both are set to high importance so reminders pop as a heads-up banner with sound — they won't sit silently in the tray.

  • Treatment reminders
  • Inspection reminders

If you've used Beekeeper's Journal before 0.3.9 you may still see older "v1" copies of these channels listed in OS settings — those don't get any new notifications and can be left alone or hidden by toggling them off.

11. QR labels

For larger operations, you can stick a printed QR label on each hive and pull it up in the app by scanning.

Print a label

  1. Open the hive you want to label.
  2. Tap the gear icon → QR label card.
  3. You'll see the QR rendered with the hive name beneath it and the encoded URI in small text (beekeepersjournal://hive/<name>).
  4. Tap Print label to launch the Android system print dialog (monochrome, portrait, fit-to-page).

Scan a label

  1. From the home screen, tap Scan hive QR.
  2. Aim at the printed label. The scanner closes automatically when it decodes a code.
  3. If the code is one of yours, the app navigates straight to that hive.

If the QR isn't one this app generated, or doesn't match any current hive, you'll see a snackbar explaining which.

Renaming a hive invalidates its printed labels. The QR encodes the hive's current name, so if you rename Apiary 1 to North Yard, the old printed label won't scan to that hive anymore. Reprint after renaming.

12. Backup & restore

Open Settings → Data backup. Two buttons: Export backup and Import backup.

What's in a backup

A backup is a ZIP file containing:

  • A manifest.json with the app version, schema version, and export timestamp.
  • The SQLite database file (every hive, inspection, harvest, feeding, treatment, and notes — committed cleanly with a WAL checkpoint).
  • All your inspection photo files.

Export

  1. Tap Export backup.
  2. Android's document picker opens with a default filename like beekeepers-journal-backup-2026-05-15.zip. Choose a location — internal storage, an SD card, Google Drive, Dropbox, etc.
  3. A snackbar confirms the size when the export completes.

Import (e.g. after a reinstall)

  1. Tap Import backup and pick the ZIP.
  2. Confirm the destructive-action dialog. Importing replaces every hive and record currently in the app.
  3. The "Backup staged" dialog appears. Tap Restart now. The app relaunches.
  4. On the next launch you'll see a toast: "Backup restored — your hives are loaded."
Why a restart? The app applies the staged backup during Application.onCreate(), before the database is opened. Restarting guarantees a fresh cold start, which is the cleanest way to swap the live DB.
Schema check. A backup made by a newer app version than this one supports will be rejected with a message. Update the app and try again. Older backups are accepted; Room migrations run automatically.

14. Privacy & permissions

Beekeeper's Journal works offline. No analytics, no advertising, no third-party tracking, no accounts. Records, photos, and locations are never transmitted off your device. The few network requests are: OpenStreetMap tile downloads when you open a map picker, and the device's built-in Geocoder when you ask for an address lookup.

Permissions the app asks for

PermissionWhenWhy
Photo libraryWhen you tap Add photo on an inspectionSystem photo picker — only the photo you choose is delivered.
ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION / COARSEWhen you tap Use my current location on a hive's Location cardReads the device's last-known location to drop a pin. Stored locally with the hive.
POST_NOTIFICATIONS (Android 13+)First launchLets the app deliver local treatment and inspection reminders. No remote push.
CAMERAWhen you tap Scan hive QRZXing decodes a single QR locally. Frames aren't saved or transmitted.

15. Troubleshooting

"I imported a backup but my data isn't there."

The backup applies on the next cold start of the app. If you didn't tap Restart now from the staged dialog, do that. If you tapped Later, fully close the app — tap Close app in the home top bar, or swipe it away from recents — then reopen. You should see a toast "Backup restored" when the apply runs. If you don't see the toast, the staged data may have been cleared; try the import again.

"Edits I made on a sub-screen don't show up on the previous screen."

This shouldn't happen as of 0.3.5 — the affected ViewModels were rewritten to observe the database reactively. If it still does, please report the screen and what you edited so I can take a look.

"Notifications don't fire."

  • Open Android Settings → Apps → Beekeeper's Journal → Notifications and confirm both Treatment reminders and Inspection reminders channels are on.
  • If your device aggressively restricts battery for the app, schedules may be delayed or dropped. Toggle off battery optimization for this app.

"My QR doesn't scan to the right hive anymore."

QRs encode the hive's current name. If you renamed the hive after printing the label, the old label won't scan to that hive. Reprint from Hive Settings → QR label.

"I deleted a hive by mistake."

There's no undo. If you have a recent backup ZIP, import it to restore everything to that point (this also replaces any data you've added since the backup).
